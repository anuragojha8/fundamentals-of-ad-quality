Appendix A

Glossary

Every term in the book, in one place, for when someone says "logQ correction" in a meeting and you need a second.

A/B test Ch. 9
A randomized experiment: users are split (usually by a hash of their ID) into control and treatment, and differences beyond chance are attributed to the change.
A/A test Ch. 9
An experiment where both arms are identical; its "lifts" show the noise floor a real effect must beat.
Ad load Ch. 14
How many ads are shown per unit of organic content; the largest short-term vs. long-term lever.
Attribution window Ch. 4
How long after a click a conversion still counts toward that ad (e.g. 7 days).
AUC Ch. 9
Area under the ROC curve: the probability a random positive is scored above a random negative. Measures ranking only, and includes cross-user pairs the auction never compares.
Autobidding Ch. 2, 12
The platform bids on the advertiser's behalf toward a goal such as a target cost per conversion.
Bid shading Ch. 2
Bidding below one's true value, which is rational in first-price auctions.
Budget pacing Ch. 15
Spreading an advertiser's spend over the day, typically by throttling participation, so the budget isn't exhausted early.
Calibration Ch. 3
Predictions match observed rates: among impressions predicted at 2%, about 2% are clicked.
Calibration layer Ch. 3
A small post-model mapping (histogram binning, Platt scaling, isotonic regression) refitted often on recent outcomes.
Calibration ratio Ch. 3
Σ predicted / Σ actual. 1.0 overall can still hide large errors in slices.
Candidate generation Ch. 1, 10
See retrieval.
Cold start Ch. 1, 6, 13
New ads, advertisers, or users with no history; ID features are untrained, and counting features are empty.
Confidence interval Ch. 9
The range of effects consistent with the data; if it contains zero, the effect isn't detectable.
Counting feature Ch. 6
A historical rate (e.g. an ad's CTR so far) supplied as a feature; must be computed only from data available at serve time.
CPA / target CPA Ch. 12
Cost per acquisition (conversion); in oCPM the advertiser states the CPA it is willing to pay.
CPA adherence Ch. 12
Whether advertisers' actual cost per conversion matches their target, the core promise of conversion bidding.
CPC / CPM Ch. 2
Cost per click / cost per thousand impressions: what the advertiser pays for.
Cross feature Ch. 6
A feature for a combination of values (e.g. hour × category) so a linear model can learn interactions.
Cross layer (DCN) Ch. 11
x₀ ⊙ (W xₗ + b) + xₗ: a layer that learns explicit feature interactions.
Cross tower Ch. 11
A model where user and ad features interact from the first layer; accurate, but can't be precomputed. Used in late ranking.
CTR / pCTR Ch. 1
Click-through rate / its prediction, usually for the top slot.
CVR / pCVR Ch. 12
Conversion rate given a click / its prediction.
Delayed feedback Ch. 4, 12
Labels (especially conversions) arrive late, so recent examples look negative until they mature.
Distillation Ch. 13
Training a small student model to imitate a larger teacher's predicted probabilities.
Drift Ch. 13
Changes in users, ads, and seasons that make a trained model stale.
Early stopping Ch. 7
Stopping training at the point where held-out loss is lowest.
eCPM Ch. 2
Expected revenue per thousand impressions: bid × pCTR × 1000 for CPC, CPA × pCTR × pCVR × 1000 for oCPM.
Eligibility Ch. 1
Hard filters applied before any model: flight dates, targeting, budgets, frequency caps, policy.
Embedding Ch. 10, 11
A learned vector representing an ID; similar entities end up close together.
Epoch Ch. 7
One pass over the training data. Large CTR models often overfit after one.
ESMM Ch. 12
Entire Space Multi-task Model: trains pCTR × pCVR on all impressions so pCVR is learned where it's used.
Examination hypothesis Ch. 5
P(click) = P(user examines the slot) × P(click | examined).
Exploration (ε) Ch. 5
Traffic deliberately spent on uncertain choices to gather information the system otherwise never sees.
Exposure bias Ch. 5, 9, 10
Models trained only on what the old system showed can't judge what it didn't; the main cause of the offline–online gap.
Feature hashing Ch. 6
Mapping feature strings to a fixed number of buckets; collisions are the price of a bounded table.
Feedback loop Ch. 5
Predictions decide what is shown, which decides the data the next model learns from.
First-price / second-price auction Ch. 2
The winner pays its own bid / the runner-up's bid. Second price makes bidding one's true value optimal (single slot).
Frequency cap Ch. 1
A limit on how often one user sees the same ad or advertiser.
GAUC Ch. 8, 9, 11
AUC computed within each request (or user) and averaged: closer to the decision the auction makes.
Gradient descent / SGD Ch. 7
Repeatedly nudging weights against the gradient of the loss; stochastic = one example or small batch per step.
GSP Ch. 2
Generalized second-price auction: rank by bid × pCTR, each winner pays the next score ÷ its own pCTR.
Guardrail metric Ch. 9, 14
A metric that must not degrade for a launch to proceed (e.g. hides, latency, advertiser value).
Head Ch. 8
A task-specific final layer on shared representations (pCTR head, pCVR head, pHide head).
Holdout (long-term) Ch. 14
A group of users kept on the old policy for months to measure slow effects like retention.
IPW Ch. 5
Inverse propensity weighting: weight each observation by 1 / probability it was observed.
Label Ch. 5
The outcome a model learns to predict; its definition is a product decision.
Latency budget Ch. 1
The time the ad server has to respond; caps model cost × number of candidates.
Leakage Ch. 6
A feature that contains information about the label that won't exist at serve time.
Learning rate Ch. 7
The step size of gradient descent.
Log loss Ch. 7, 8
−[y log p + (1−y) log(1−p)]; the standard loss for calibrated probabilities.
Logit Ch. 7
The raw score z before the sigmoid; log-odds.
Multi-task learning Ch. 8
Several heads and losses trained on shared layers; task weights are tradeoffs.
Negative downsampling Ch. 4
Keeping a fraction w of negatives; correct predictions with q = p / (p + (1−p)/w).
Negative transfer Ch. 8
When tasks sharing layers degrade each other.
Normalized entropy (NE) Ch. 9
Log loss ÷ entropy of the base rate. Below 1 beats predicting the average; captures ranking and calibration.
oCPM Ch. 2, 12
Optimized CPM: the advertiser bids per conversion, and the platform prices with pCTR × pCVR.
Offline–online gap Ch. 9
Offline metrics on logged data disagree with online results, often because of exposure bias.
Oracle Ch. 1
In this book: a simulator-only policy using the true probabilities, used to measure headroom.
Overfitting Ch. 7
Fitting noise: training loss falls while held-out loss rises.
Pairwise loss Ch. 8
A loss on the order of two items; good for ranking, useless for pricing on its own.
pHide Ch. 14
Predicted probability a user hides or reports an ad.
Poisson loss Ch. 8
λ − y log λ for count targets; approximately log loss for rare events.
Position bias Ch. 5
Lower slots get fewer clicks regardless of the ad, because fewer users look.
Pre-training / fine-tuning Ch. 13
Learn general representations from abundant signals, then adapt them to the sparse task.
Quality penalty (λ) Ch. 14
A term like λ · pHide subtracted from the auction score; the exchange rate between annoyance and revenue.
Recall@K Ch. 10
Share of the truly best items that appear in a stage's top K.
Regularization (L2) Ch. 7
A pull toward zero that tames weights backed by little data.
Reliability diagram Ch. 3
Binned predicted vs. actual rates; a calibrated model lies on the diagonal.
Reserve price Ch. 2
A minimum eCPM to be shown; trades fill rate for price, and matters most in thin markets.
Retrieval Ch. 1, 10
The cheap first stage that cuts millions of ads to hundreds of candidates.
Sample selection bias Ch. 12
Training on one population (clicks) and serving on another (all impressions).
Sampled softmax Ch. 10
The clicked item competes against k sampled negatives; the standard retrieval loss.
Slice Ch. 9
A segment-level metric (by country, category, ad age, bid type).
Smoothing Ch. 6
Shrinking small-sample rates toward a prior: (c + p·m)/(n + m).
Target attention Ch. 11
Weighting history items by their similarity to the candidate ad (as in DIN).
Train/serve skew Ch. 4
Features differ between training and serving, often because they were recomputed after the fact.
Truthfulness Ch. 2
An auction property: bidding one's true value is optimal.
Two-tower model Ch. 10
Separate user and ad networks whose outputs meet in a dot product; ad vectors can be precomputed and indexed.
VCG Ch. 2
Vickrey–Clarke–Groves: each winner pays the harm it causes others; truthful with multiple slots.
Warm start Ch. 13
Continue training from existing weights on new data rather than from scratch.
Wide & Deep Ch. 11
A linear model over engineered features plus a deep network, trained jointly.
