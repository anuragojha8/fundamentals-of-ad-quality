Run the Marketplace

In which I get the keys to the whole marketplace and try very hard not to crash it.

Okay. Deep breath. You're the Ads Quality PM at Pebble, and the next quarter is yours.

Everything you built in this book is now live: your eligibility filter, your auction, your features, your models, your retrieval, your quality penalty. All of it. Each simulated week, Pebble's world keeps moving: interests drift, new ads launch, budgets get spent, users get a little happier or a little more annoyed. You make the calls a real quality PM makes, and then you live with the results. Advertisers, users, and a finance team are all watching.

17.1How the quarter works

  • Each week is one simulated day of Pebble traffic, served by your stack with whatever levers you've set. The world jumps forward five days per week, so drift is very real.
  • Advertisers have budgets. They're sized from a pre-season week, so the bigger spenders will run into them. Once an advertiser's budget runs out, it stops bidding for the rest of the day, unless pacing spreads its spend out.
  • Advertisers leave if their return on ad spend (conversion value ÷ spend) stays below 0.85 for three weeks in a row. Overcharge people and they walk. Just like real life.
  • Users react slowly. Pebble's active users grow about 1% a week on their own, and shrink with ad load and hides (a gentler version of the Chapter 15 toy model: weekly loss = 0.4% × ad slots + 40% × hides per request). Fewer users next week means less traffic to monetize.
  • You're graded against autopilot. A shadow Pebble runs the exact same weeks with the default levers (heuristic ranker, 3 ad slots, no pacing, no penalties). Your job: beat it on revenue, users, and advertiser value, without losing more advertisers than it does. It's the same logic as a long-term holdout, which you now know all about.
  • Everything is measured against the truth: expected revenue, conversions, and hides, not noisy coin flips. Real life will not be this kind to you. Enjoy it.

17.2After the quarter

How'd it go? However it went, every lever in that game is one you've studied, and almost all of them trade something now for something later. Here are some patterns strong players tend to find:

  • Calibration is an advertiser-retention problem. Switching to oCPM with a miscalibrated pCVR can juice revenue early and quietly lose you advertisers a few weeks later (Chapters 3 and 12).
  • Budgets cap revenue. Once advertisers hit their budgets, a better ranker shows up more as advertiser value than as revenue. Pacing rarely hurts, and it helps most when budgets really bind (without it, big spenders drop out early and the rest of the day turns into one of Chapter 2's thin markets).
  • Stale models decay quietly. Warm-start retraining on recent weeks keeps your model honest as the world drifts (Chapter 14).
  • Ad load is a slow-motion tradeoff. An extra slot wins every single week-one revenue comparison, and sends the bill to user health (Chapter 15).
  • The first bit of quality penalty is often free (Chapter 15). And exploration is an investment whose returns only land if something learns from it (Chapter 5).

If you didn't win on your first try, good. That means the game is doing its job. Change one lever at a time, and watch which metric actually moves. That habit, more than any model, is the job.

Write your end-of-quarter review for Pebble's leadership, in five bullet points: what you changed, what happened to revenue, advertisers, and users, what you'd do differently, and what you'd put on next quarter's roadmap.

A strong review is short, quantified, and honest about tradeoffs. Something like:

  • What we did: shipped the LR ranker with weekly warm-start retraining, turned on budget pacing in week 2, and set a λ = 1 hide penalty.
  • Revenue: +X% vs. the autopilot holdout over the quarter; most of the gain came after retraining kicked in (weeks 3–12).
  • Advertisers: N of M retained, vs. M′ on autopilot. Two churned in weeks 4–5 after we tested oCPM; we rolled back in week 6. Root cause: pCVR miscalibration for slow-converting verticals (delayed feedback).
  • Users: active-user index +Y% vs. autopilot. Hides −10% after the quality penalty; ad load held at 3.
  • Next quarter: delay-corrected pCVR before retrying oCPM; a long-term holdout for ad load; more exploration budget to feed the deep ranker's data needs.
