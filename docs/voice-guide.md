# Voice guide: book-specific rules

Follow `ANURAG_WRITING_GUIDE.md` (in the My Projects folder) for all prose in this book: chapter text, callouts, quiz explanations, widget captions. The rules below add to it and win where the two conflict. Code comments and test messages stay plain.

## Narrator
- The book is Anurag walking a friend through his stack, not a textbook narrator.
- Where a real work story would land, leave `<!-- ANURAG: … -->` with a prompt describing what kind of story fits. Never fill it in yourself.

## Percy Jackson, applied
- **Chapter subtitles (the dek under each title)** follow Riordan's chapter-title style: "In which I…", first person, slightly absurd, and literally true about the chapter. Example: "In which I accidentally run three thousand auctions before breakfast." The plain `<h1>` stays for navigation.
- The deadpan, warning, and hyperbole moves from the main guide apply throughout, but keep them out of derivations and metric definitions.

## Audience
- Product managers with a CS background, reading at work. **No profanity or dating material.** Keep the energy, stay workplace-shareable.

## Structure
- **Open** each chapter mid-thought: "Okay, so…", "You know, I think…".
- **End** each chapter with a callback to its opening image and a light sign-off pointing to the next chapter.
- Keep the callout types (Intuition, In your job, Common trap, Going deeper), written in voice.
- Pebble is the running example.

## Precision
- Formulas, exact numbers, and terminology are never sacrificed for a joke.
- Keep teaching simulations separate from empirical claims about real ad systems.

## Sentence rhythm
- Avoid stacking short fragments to manufacture emphasis, especially in instructions and quiz explanations. Connect the action to its reason: what changes, why it matters, and what the reader should try next.
- Keep a short sentence when it earns its place as a punchline or a clear landing after a developed thought. Do not flatten every paragraph into one long sentence.
